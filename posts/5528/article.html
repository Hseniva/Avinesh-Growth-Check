What is first-party data?

First-party data is information you collect directly from your own audience, on your own channels.

Common examples include:

  • Email addresses and phone numbers from sign-ups
  • Purchase and order history
  • Website and app behaviour, such as pages viewed and products added to basket
  • Survey answers and enquiry form details
  • Engagement with your emails, WhatsApp messages and social posts

Because it comes straight from your customers, it is more accurate than anything you can buy. And when it is collected with clear consent, it is far more privacy-friendly.

First-party vs second-party vs third-party data

Second-party data

Another company's first-party data, shared through a partnership. It can help you reach new audiences, but you depend on your partner's data quality and consent practices.

Third-party data

Collected by external companies, such as data brokers, and sold to marketers. It offers scale, but accuracy is low and privacy rules keep tightening.

There is also a fourth type worth knowing: data customers tell you on purpose, like preferences and budgets. I cover it in my zero-party data strategy guide.

Why first-party data matters more in 2026

Let me clear up a common myth first. Google did not phase out third-party cookies in Chrome.

In July 2024, Google said that "instead of deprecating third-party cookies" it would give Chrome users a choice. In April 2025, it confirmed it would keep its current approach.

But that doesn't mean tracking is fine. Here is why first-party data is still essential:

  • Browsers block cookies: Safari has fully blocked third-party cookies by default since 2020, and Firefox made Total Cookie Protection the default in 2022.
  • Privacy laws require consent: GDPR in Europe and Mauritius's Data Protection Act 2017, which was designed to align with GDPR, require a lawful basis such as clear consent.
  • Customers expect relevance: McKinsey found 71% of consumers expect personalised interactions.
  • AI-driven ad platforms need good inputs: automated bidding learns from the conversion data you send it. Weak signals mean weak optimisation.

How to start collecting first-party data: 6 steps

Step 1: Fix your tracking foundation

Set up Google Analytics 4, Google Tag Manager and a consent banner that actually works. If your conversions aren't tracked properly, nothing else on this list will perform.

Step 2: Offer a clear value exchange

People share data when they get something back. Offer a discount, a useful guide, early access or a free consultation in return for an email or phone number.

Step 3: Use forms, quizzes and surveys

Keep forms short. Add one or two questions that help you segment, such as "What are you looking for?" or "When do you plan to buy?"

Step 4: Launch a loyalty or rewards programme

Loyalty schemes give customers a reason to identify themselves at every purchase, online and in store.

Step 5: Centralise everything in a CRM

Bring website, shop, email and ad data into one CRM or customer data platform. One customer, one record, one source of truth.

Step 6: Connect it back to your ad platforms

Sync audiences and conversions with Meta and Google automatically. Then test, measure and refine every month.

Frequently asked questions

What is first-party data in marketing?

First-party data is information a business collects directly from its own customers and audience, such as email addresses, purchase history, website behaviour and survey answers, on channels it owns.

Did Google stop third-party cookies in Chrome?

No. In July 2024 Google dropped its plan to deprecate third-party cookies in Chrome and confirmed in April 2025 that it would keep its current approach. Safari and Firefox block third-party cookies by default.

How does first-party data improve Facebook and Google Ads?

It lets you build audiences from real customers, exclude existing buyers, create better lookalikes and send more complete conversion data through tools like the Meta Conversions API and Google Enhanced Conversions, which helps automated bidding optimise.

Is collecting first-party data legal in Mauritius?

Yes, if you follow the Data Protection Act 2017. You need a lawful basis such as clear, informed consent, and customers have the right to object to direct marketing.
