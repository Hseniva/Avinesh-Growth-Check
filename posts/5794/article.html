What GEO actually means

Generative Engine Optimisation is the practice of making your content easy for AI systems to find, understand, trust and cite.

The term comes from a 2024 paper by researchers at Princeton and partners, presented at the KDD conference. They found GEO methods can boost visibility in generative engine responses by up to 40%.

Traditional SEO asks: "How do I rank?" GEO asks: "How do I become the source the AI quotes?"

GEO is not a replacement for SEO

In May 2026, Google published guidance stating that "optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." It also listed llms.txt files, content "chunking" and special schema as things you don't need for AI Overviews or AI Mode.

So be wary of anyone selling GEO as a magic trick. The foundations are crawlable pages, genuine expertise and clear answers.

The four pillars of effective GEO

1. Be crawlable by AI systems

If AI tools can't access your site, they can't cite it. For example, OpenAI's documentation says OAI-SearchBot is the crawler used to surface websites in ChatGPT search. Check your robots.txt and firewall aren't blocking the bots you want.

2. Entity-based content

AI systems think in entities: people, brands, places and services. Describe yours consistently everywhere, from your website and LinkedIn to Google Business Profile and directories. Schema markup such as Organization and Person won't guarantee AI visibility, but it helps machines connect the dots and supports rich results.

3. Direct answer content

Lead with the answer, then expand. Long introductions that bury the point are hard to quote. Use question-based headings and answer each one in the first sentence or two.

4. Citation and credibility signals

The Princeton research found that adding citations, quotations from relevant sources and statistics helped most. Combine that with real expertise and mentions on credible third-party sites. This is where E-E-A-T and human-first content pay off.

7 practical GEO actions for 2026

  1. Ask the AI about yourself. Search your brand and your core services in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode. Note who gets cited instead of you.
  2. Check AI crawler access. Review robots.txt, your CDN and any security plugins that might block AI search bots.
  3. Rewrite your top 20 pages answer-first. Put a clear one or two sentence answer under each heading.
  4. Add sources and data. Back claims with linked, verifiable statistics and quotes from credible sources.
  5. Build an entity-style About page. Who you are, what you do, credentials, notable clients and locations, written consistently with your other profiles.
  6. Earn third-party mentions. Industry publications, podcasts, reviews and partner sites all help AI systems trust you.
  7. Track AI referrals. In GA4, watch referral traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com, and log your AI citations monthly.

Frequently asked questions

What is Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)?

GEO is the practice of making your content easy for AI systems like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity to find, understand, trust and cite in their answers.

Is GEO different from SEO?

GEO builds on SEO rather than replacing it. Google says optimising for its AI features is still SEO. GEO adds a stronger focus on direct answers, entities, sources and being cited across AI platforms.

Do I need llms.txt or special schema for AI search?

Not for Google. Its May 2026 guidance says llms.txt and special structured data are not required for AI Overviews or AI Mode. Schema can still help with rich results and entity clarity.

How do I know if AI tools are citing my brand?

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the questions your customers ask, and record who is cited. Then track AI referral traffic in GA4 every month.
