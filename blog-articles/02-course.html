What to look for in a digital marketing course in Mauritius

Digital marketing changes fast. A course that was excellent three years ago can be quietly out of date today.

Here are the six things I'd check before paying for any programme.

1. Practical work in real ad accounts

You don't learn Meta or Google Ads by reading about them. You learn by setting up a campaign, choosing an audience, watching the numbers move and fixing what doesn't work.

Ask the provider a simple question: will I build and manage campaigns in a real ad account during the course? If the answer is vague, that tells you a lot.

If you want a taste of what that hands-on work looks like, my guide to mastering Facebook Ads Manager is a good starting point.

2. Up-to-date AI content

AI is no longer a bonus module. It now shapes how ads are bought, how content is written and how campaigns are optimised.

A modern course should show you how to use AI tools in your daily work, and also where they go wrong. Look for topics like AI agents in campaign management, automated bidding and generative search, not just "how to write a prompt".

3. A trainer with real industry experience

There's a big difference between someone who teaches marketing and someone who does marketing every day.

Ask who will actually train you. Do they manage live budgets? Do they deal with real clients, real tracking problems and real platform changes? The best trainers bring last week's lessons into this week's class.

4. HRDC refundable and MQA approved status

If your employer is paying, this one matters a lot.

According to the HRDC eligibility rules, only employers contributing monthly to the training levy can claim, and only courses that have received the prior approval of the Mauritius Qualifications Authority (or the Higher Education Commission) qualify. The training must also be job-related and lead to new skills.

The refund itself depends on the employer's levy. HRDC's rate of refund page lists 70 to 75% of training costs for most employers, and up to 90% for courses explicitly focused on AI (up to Diploma level) from 1 July 2024, subject to the grant ceiling and scheme limits.

Always confirm the exact amount for your case directly with HRDC, and check a provider's approval status with the MQA.

5. A recognised certificate

A certificate won't make you a great marketer on its own. But a recognised one shows employers and clients that the programme met an official standard.

In Mauritius, MQA approval is the signal to look for, and as you've just seen, it's also what unlocks HRDC refunds for employers.

6. Support and feedback

Learning stalls when you get stuck and nobody notices.

Ask how feedback works. Is it one generic comment at the end, or regular, personal feedback on your actual work? Can you track your progress between sessions? Small details like these decide whether you finish strong or quietly drop off.

Before any digital marketing course in Mauritius, take a pre-assessment

Here's the part most people skip, and it's the one I care about most.

Not all students learn the same way. Some people need to see a diagram before anything clicks. Others want a clear, step-by-step structure. Some learn best by talking ideas through with others. And some only really understand something once they've done it with their own hands.

Put all four types in the same course, at the same pace, with the same feedback, and at least some of them will struggle. Not because they lack talent, but because the format doesn't fit them.

Starting level matters too. A business owner who already runs Facebook ads and a graduate who has never opened Ads Manager need very different first weeks.

That's why I believe every student should take a pre-assessment before choosing a course. It turns a guess into an informed decision.
