The rise of AI search tools

Your readers no longer just type three keywords into Google. They ask full questions and expect a full answer.

The scale is huge:

  • ChatGPT: 900 million weekly active users, announced by OpenAI in February 2026 (TechCrunch).
  • Google AI Mode: over 1 billion monthly active users, and the Gemini app has 950 million, according to Alphabet's July 2026 earnings call.
  • Fewer clicks: Ahrefs found AI Overviews reduce the click-through rate of the top-ranking page by about 58%.

For writers, that means your audience is increasingly meeting your work through an AI summary, not your page.

How AI search tools actually find and use content

The main tools you need to understand are Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude with web search. They differ in detail, but most follow the same pattern.

1. They split one question into many searches

Google describes AI Mode's "query fan-out" as "breaking down your question into subtopics and issuing a multitude of queries simultaneously". A page that covers the main question and its natural follow-ups has more chances to be picked.

2. They need to be able to crawl your page

Each tool relies on a search index and crawlers. For example, OpenAI says its OAI-SearchBot crawler is what surfaces websites in ChatGPT search. Block it, and your pages won't appear in those answers.

3. They quote clear, trustworthy passages

AI tools pull the passages that answer a question cleanly and back it up. Research behind Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) found that adding citations, quotations and statistics improved visibility in AI answers by up to 40%.

What this means for how you write

Here's the part many writers get wrong. You don't need a special "AI style".

In May 2026, Google published guidance saying that optimising for its AI search features "is still SEO". It specifically listed llms.txt files, chunking content for AI and "AI-friendly" writing as unnecessary.

What does change is your focus:

  • From keywords to questions. Write for the full question a reader would ask, including follow-ups.
  • From padding to answers. Give the answer first, then the detail.
  • From generic to first-hand. Add your own experience, examples and opinions. This is what E-E-A-T rewards and what AI can't copy.
  • From claims to evidence. Link every statistic to its original source.

How to use AI tools in your writing workflow: 6 steps

Step 1: Research the questions, not just the keyword

Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the question your article targets. Note the sub-questions they cover and the sources they cite. Then check keyword tools like Ahrefs or Semrush for search demand.

Step 2: Build an outline around the reader's journey

Turn those sub-questions into H2 and H3 headings. Use AI to suggest gaps, but decide the structure yourself.

Step 3: Draft fast, then make it yours

AI can help you get a first draft down. But rewrite it in your voice and add what only you know: client stories, tests you ran, mistakes you've seen. Make sure you follow your company's AI usage policy.

Step 4: Fact-check every number

AI tools can invent statistics or mix up sources. Open the original study for every figure and link to it. If you can't verify it, cut it.

Step 5: Make the page easy to quote

Put a short, direct answer under each heading. Add an FAQ section, descriptive image alt text and an author bio that shows real credentials.

Step 6: Track AI visibility, not just rankings

In GA4, watch referral traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com. Re-ask your target questions in AI tools every month and log whether you're cited. Update the pages that aren't.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Publishing raw AI drafts. They sound like everything else and add nothing new for an AI tool to cite.
  • Chasing "AI hacks". Special files and formatting tricks are no substitute for genuinely helpful content.
  • Blocking the crawlers you need. Check robots.txt and security plugins before wondering why you're never cited.
  • Copying unverified statistics. One wrong number can undermine trust in the whole article.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI search tools should content writers understand?

Focus on Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Gemini. They reach the largest audiences and all build answers from web content.

Do I need to write differently for AI search?

Not in a special "AI style". Google says optimising for its AI features is still SEO. Focus on answering full questions clearly, adding first-hand experience and linking to sources.

Can I use AI to write my SEO content?

You can use AI to research, outline and draft, but a human should edit, fact-check and add real experience. Unedited AI content rarely offers anything new for readers or AI tools to cite.

How can I tell if AI tools are using my content?

Check GA4 for referrals from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com, and regularly ask AI tools your target questions to see whether your pages are cited.
