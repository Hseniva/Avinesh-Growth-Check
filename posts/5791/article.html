What is an AI agent in campaign management?

Automation follows rules you write. "If CPA goes above €20, pause the ad."

An AI agent is different. You give it a goal, and it decides how to reach it. It learns from every auction and adjusts on its own.

Google Performance Max and Meta Advantage+ are the two systems most advertisers already use. Both now handle targeting, placements, bidding, creative combinations and budget allocation with very little manual input.

And it is going further. At Google Marketing Live 2025, Google announced agentic tools for Google Ads and Analytics that recommend keywords and creative, and can apply changes for you. Google is also pushing AI into Search campaigns, which I covered in my guide to Google AI Max for Search.

What AI agents actually do in your campaigns

  • Predict conversion likelihood for each impression before the bid is placed.
  • Mix and match creative from your headlines, images and videos, and favour what performs.
  • Move budget between channels, placements and audiences in real time.
  • Find new audiences beyond the ones you would have targeted manually.

No human team can make these decisions at the same speed or scale. That is the real shift.

The performance data behind the shift

Here is what Google and Meta have published about their AI campaign systems:

  • Google Performance Max: 27% more conversions or value on average at a similar CPA or ROAS, based on Google data from October to November 2023 for advertisers outside retail, according to Google.
  • Meta Advantage+: an average of $4.52 in revenue for every dollar spent, a 22% increase compared with business-as-usual campaigns, as shared by Meta in April 2025.
  • Advantage+ sales campaigns: 9% lower cost per action on average with Meta's streamlined setup, from the same Meta update.
  • Advantage+ leads campaigns: 10% lower cost per qualified lead in early testing, according to Meta.

A word of honesty. These are the platforms' own averages. Some accounts beat them. Some do worse, especially with poor tracking or thin data.

And not every "AI agent" deserves the label. Gartner predicts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 because of rising costs, unclear business value or weak risk controls. It also warns about "agent washing", where vendors rebrand basic tools as agents.

The lesson: trust the tested platform systems, but question every shiny new tool.

What this means for marketers

AI agents are replacing the execution layer. Manual bid changes, endless audience splits and daily budget tweaks are fading out.

What they cannot replace is judgement. Which product to push. Which margin matters. What message fits your brand. When the algorithm is optimising for the wrong thing.

Gartner expects at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions to be made autonomously through agentic AI by 2028. The marketers who thrive will be the ones directing those decisions, not competing with them.

How to adapt: 4 steps I use with every account

Step 1: Simplify your campaign structure

AI needs data to learn. Splitting budget across dozens of small campaigns and ad sets starves it.

Consolidate. Fewer campaigns, broader audiences, more conversions per campaign. I explain the Meta side in detail in my Facebook Ads Manager guide.

Step 2: Feed the AI better inputs

The agent is only as good as what you give it. Focus on three inputs:

  • Clean conversion tracking: Meta Conversions API, Google enhanced conversions and offline conversion imports.
  • First-party data: customer lists and CRM data. Here is why first-party data matters.
  • Creative variety: different formats, angles, hooks and offers.

Step 3: Optimise for business outcomes

If you tell the AI to chase clicks, it will find cheap clicks. Not customers.

Set goals around revenue, profit or qualified leads. Use value-based bidding where you can, and pass lead quality back from your CRM.

Step 4: Build a testing framework

Do not accept platform averages on faith. Run structured tests: AI campaign against your current setup, one creative angle against another, one bid strategy against another.

Document what you learn. Over time, that playbook becomes your competitive advantage.

Frequently asked questions

Will AI agents replace PPC managers?

They are replacing manual execution, such as bid changes and audience splits. Strategy, data setup, creative direction and testing still need a human.

Does Performance Max really perform better?

Google reports 27% more conversions or value on average at a similar CPA or ROAS for advertisers who adopt it. Results depend on your tracking, data and creative, so test it against your current setup.

What results does Meta report for Advantage+?

Meta says advertisers using Advantage+ generate an average of $4.52 in revenue for every dollar spent, a 22% increase over business-as-usual campaigns.

How do I get started with AI-managed campaigns?

Fix your conversion tracking first, consolidate your campaigns, supply plenty of creative variations and set goals based on revenue or qualified leads rather than clicks.
