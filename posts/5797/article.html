What is hyper-personalisation?

Traditional personalisation groups people into segments. "Women, 25 to 34, interested in fitness." Everyone in that bucket gets the same message.

Hyper-personalisation shrinks the segment to one person. AI uses real-time behaviour, context and purchase history to decide what each individual sees, and when.

Ten years ago, that needed a team of data scientists. Today, it is built into the platforms you already use: Meta, Google, your email tool and your ecommerce platform.

How hyper-personalisation works: the three engines

Every hyper-personalisation system I set up for clients comes down to three parts working together.

1. Signal collection

The AI needs to know what each person is doing. Pages viewed, products added to basket, emails opened, time of day, device, location.

The best signals are the ones you own. That is why a solid first-party data strategy comes first.

2. Predictive intent modelling

Next, the AI predicts what each person is likely to do. Buy now? Churn? Need a reminder? Respond to a discount, or to social proof?

3. Dynamic creative delivery

Finally, the system assembles the right message from your building blocks: headline, image, offer and call to action. It then delivers it on the right channel at the right moment.

One warning. Bad signals produce bad personalisation. Showing someone an ad for a product they bought yesterday does more harm than a generic message.

Where hyper-personalisation pays off fastest

Paid social ads

Meta's Advantage+ creative adapts your assets for different people and placements. Advantage+ catalog ads (formerly dynamic product ads) show each user the products they are most likely to want. Give it variety and it does the matching for you.

Email and CRM

Send-time optimisation, dynamic content blocks and AI-written subject line variants are now standard in most email tools.

Measure clicks and revenue rather than open rates. Apple's Mail Privacy Protection has made opens an unreliable metric.

Website and landing pages

Returning visitors can see different hero banners, recommended products or case studies based on what they looked at last time. A first-time visitor from a Google ad and a loyal customer should not see the same homepage.

Conversational AI

AI assistants that remember context can guide each customer like your best salesperson would. But they need a human handoff. I covered why in my article on how AI chatbots talk shoppers out of buying.

How to start: 5 steps

Step 1: Audit your data

List every place you collect customer data: website, CRM, email, ecommerce, WhatsApp. Check your tracking works. Fix gaps before you add any AI on top.

Step 2: Collect first-party and zero-party data

Build email lists, preference centres, quizzes and loyalty programmes. Data customers give you directly is the most accurate signal you can get. My guide to zero-party data shows you how.

Step 3: Build modular creative

Stop producing one perfect ad. Produce building blocks instead: five headlines, five visuals, three offers, three calls to action. AI can only personalise what you give it.

Step 4: Start with one channel

Pick the channel with the most data, usually email or Meta ads. Switch on the built-in personalisation features, run a controlled test and compare against your current baseline.

Step 5: Measure incrementally

Last-click attribution hides what personalisation really does. Use holdout groups and incrementality tests to see the true lift.

3 mistakes I see all the time

  • Personalising on stale data. Retargeting someone with a product they already bought tells them you are not paying attention. Exclude recent buyers.
  • Too few creative variations. One image and one headline give the algorithm nothing to choose from. Personalisation needs options.
  • Judging it on vanity metrics. Higher clicks mean little if revenue stays flat. Tie every test to sales, leads or customer lifetime value.

Privacy by design

There is a fine line between helpful and creepy. Ask for explicit consent, explain what you do with data and give people a clear benefit in return.

If your team uses AI tools to process customer data, set clear rules first. Start with a written AI usage policy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between personalisation and hyper-personalisation?

Personalisation tailors messages to segments, such as an age group or location. Hyper-personalisation uses AI and real-time behaviour to tailor messages to each individual.

Does personalisation actually increase revenue?

Yes. McKinsey found that personalisation most often drives a 10 to 15% revenue lift, with company-specific results ranging from 5% to 25%.

Can small businesses use hyper-personalisation?

Yes. Meta Advantage+, email platforms and ecommerce tools now include AI personalisation features. The main requirements are clean first-party data and enough creative variations.

Is hyper-personalisation compliant with privacy laws?

It can be, if you collect data with clear consent, explain how you use it and let people opt out. Build privacy into the system from the start rather than adding it later.
